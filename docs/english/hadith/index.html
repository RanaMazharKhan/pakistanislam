📜 Hadith — The Sayings and Actions of the Prophet ﷺ

Hadith are the recorded sayings, actions and approvals of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, preserved with their chains of narration. Below are the six most famous collections — known together as al-Sihah as-Sittah (the Six Authentic Books) — presented complete, chapter by chapter, with the Arabic text and an English translation of every hadith.

📚 The Complete Books of Hadith — Read Online

Sahih al-Bukhari

Compiled by Imam Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari (d. 256 AH) — regarded as the most authentic book after the Quran; its hadiths are arranged under topical chapter headings.

7589 hadiths · 97 books (chapters)

Read Sahih al-Bukhari →
Sahih Muslim

Compiled by Imam Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj (d. 261 AH) — named alongside Sahih al-Bukhari in authenticity; together they are called “the two Sahihs”.

7563 hadiths · 57 books (chapters)

Read Sahih Muslim →
Sunan Abu Dawud

The book of Imam Abu Dawud as-Sijistani (d. 275 AH) — a major collection of hadiths on juristic and practical rulings.

5274 hadiths · 43 books (chapters)

Read Sunan Abu Dawud →
Jami' at-Tirmidhi

The book of Imam Abu Isa at-Tirmidhi (d. 279 AH) — known for stating the grade of every hadith (sahih, hasan, gharib…).

3998 hadiths · 49 books (chapters)

Read Jami' at-Tirmidhi →
Sunan an-Nasa'i

The book of Imam Ahmad ibn Shu'ayb an-Nasa'i (d. 303 AH) — famous for its strictness in examining chains of narration.

5765 hadiths · 51 books (chapters)

Read Sunan an-Nasa'i →
Sunan Ibn Majah

The book of Imam Muhammad ibn Yazid Ibn Majah (d. 273 AH) — one of the six major books of hadith (al-Sihah as-Sittah).

4343 hadiths · 38 books (chapters)

Read Sunan Ibn Majah →

Source: the Arabic texts and English translations are taken from a free, public-domain open hadith dataset (fawazahmed0/hadith-api). Hadith numbers follow that source edition; printed editions may differ slightly in numbering. Where the English translation is not available in the source, only the Arabic text is shown, and this is noted on the card.
