🎵 Naats — Poetry in Praise of the Prophet (peace be upon him)

The Prophet's Mosque, Madinah — Photo: Wikimedia Commons (Creative Commons)

Expressing one's love for the Prophet (peace be upon him) in verse is an old and living tradition of this Ummah. On this page you will learn what a naat is, how the tradition of naat grew through history, and what decorum belongs to the gatherings where naats are recited.

What Is a Naat?

A naat is poetry written in praise of the Prophet (peace be upon him) — his noble character, his beauty and stature, his life and his miracles — which is recited as an expression of love and devotion. The praise of Allah is called hamd, and the virtue described in the naat is, in the end, a reflection of Allah's own blessing and favour.

The History of the Naat — From the First Companion to Today

The tradition of the naat begins with the era of the Prophet (peace be upon him) himself: the famous Companion Hassan bin Thabit (may Allah be pleased with him) used to write poetry in the Prophet's praise and recite it in his blessed presence, and so he is remembered as the poet of the Prophet (peace be upon him). After that, this tradition spread into Arabic, Persian, Turkish and Urdu. The most famous among them is Imam Busiri's Qasida Burdah Sharif (Egypt, 13th century), which has been translated into dozens of languages and is recited all over the world. In Urdu, the tradition of the naat reached its height in the Subcontinent, and today naats are recited in every language.

The Difference Between Hamd and Naat

HamdNaat
Praise and glorification of Allah AlmightyPraise of the Prophet (peace be upon him)
Mention of Allah's names and attributesMention of the Prophet's noble character, stature and miracles
Example: Surah Al-Fatihah (in the Quran itself)Example: Qasida Burdah, the naats of Hassan bin Thabit (may Allah be pleased with him)

Gatherings of Naat

Gatherings of naat are held in masjids, homes and schools; their most beautiful settings are the gatherings of Milad, the month of Ramadan, and the precious nights of Rabi al-Awwal. In a gathering of naat, decorum is kept, the naat khawan (reciter) stays within the limits of reverence, and the audience listens in respect and sends durood.

A Note on Famous Naats

Famous naats (such as "Mustafa Jaan-e-Rehmat" and "Hasbi Rabbi Jallallah") are the copyright of their poets, so their words are not reproduced on this website. These naats can be listened to on licensed platforms and official channels; this page merely provides information about the tradition of the naat.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a naat?

A naat is poetry written in praise of the Prophet (peace be upon him) — his noble character, his beauty and stature, his life and his miracles — which is recited as an expression of love and devotion.

What is the difference between a hamd and a naat?

A hamd is the praise and glorification of Allah Almighty, while a naat is praise of the Prophet (peace be upon him). These two colours are like the twin gates of Islamic literature.

Who was the first naat poet?

The famous Companion-poet Hassan bin Thabit (may Allah be pleased with him) is known as the poet of the Prophet (peace be upon him), and his naats are recorded in the books of Seerah and Hadith.

Which is the most famous naat in the world?

Imam Busiri's Qasida Burdah Sharif (Egypt, 13th century) is the world's most widely read naat; it has been translated into dozens of languages and is recited on every continent.

Where can famous naats be read or listened to?

Famous naats are the copyright of their poets, so their words are not reproduced on this website. For listening, use licensed and official platforms and channels.
