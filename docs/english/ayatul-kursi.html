🕌 Ayatul Kursi — the Greatest Verse of the Quran

Ayatul Kursi is verse 255 of Surah Al-Baqarah. The Prophet (peace be upon him) called it the greatest verse of the Quran. In a single verse it gathers Allah's Oneness, His life, His knowledge and His dominion in a way that fills the heart of the one who recites it with awe of Allah.

Ayatul Kursi — Arabic text
ٱللَّهُ لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ٱلْحَىُّ ٱلْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُۥ سِنَةٌۭ وَلَا نَوْمٌۭ ۚ لَّهُۥ مَا فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَمَا فِى ٱلْأَرْضِ ۗ مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُۥٓ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِۦ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَىْءٍۢ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِۦٓ إِلَّا بِمَا شَآءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَـُٔودُهُۥ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ ٱلْعَلِىُّ ٱلْعَظِيمُ

Transliteration: Allahu laaa ilaaha illaa Huwal Haiyul Qaiyoom; laa taakhuzuhoo sinatunw wa laa nawm; lahoo maa fissamaawaati wa maa fil ard; man zallazee yashfa'u indahooo illaa bi-iznih; ya'lamu maa baina aideehim wa maa khalfahum; wa laa yuheetoona bishai'im min 'ilmihee illaa bimaa shaaa'; wasi'a Kursiyyuhus samaawaati wal arda wa laa ya'ooduho hifzuhumaa; wa Huwal Aliyyul 'Azeem

Translation (Saheeh International): Allah - there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of [all] existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is [presently] before them and what will be after them, and they encompass not a thing of His knowledge except for what He wills. His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.

Reference: The Holy Quran, Surah Al-Baqarah (2:255) — Arabic text in the Uthmani script.

Why is Ayatul Kursi so special?

The verse gives a complete picture of Allah's Being and attributes: He alone is worthy of worship, He is Ever-Living and the Sustainer of all existence; neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him; whatever is in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him; none can intercede with Him except by His permission; His knowledge surrounds everything; and His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth. It is this comprehensiveness that made the Prophet (peace be upon him) call it the greatest verse of the Quran (Sahih Muslim).

Virtues of Ayatul Kursi

Before sleeping: whoever recites Ayatul Kursi before going to sleep at night has an angel appointed by Allah to guard him, and Shaytan does not come near him until morning (Sahih al-Bukhari).

After every obligatory prayer: whoever recites Ayatul Kursi after every obligatory prayer has nothing standing between him and entering Paradise except death (Sunan an-Nasa'i).

Protection of the home: Shaytan flees from the house in which Surah Al-Baqarah is recited (Sahih Muslim) — and Ayatul Kursi is the greatest verse of that surah.

When to recite Ayatul Kursi

After every obligatory prayer, before sleeping, in the morning and evening, when leaving the home, and in times of fear or worry — make reciting Ayatul Kursi a daily habit.

Common Questions

Which verse of the Quran is Ayatul Kursi?

Ayatul Kursi is verse 255 of Surah Al-Baqarah. Its name comes from the words 'His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth.'

What are the benefits of reciting Ayatul Kursi?

Whoever recites it after every obligatory prayer has nothing standing between him and Paradise except death (Sunan an-Nasa'i), and whoever recites it before sleeping is guarded by an angel appointed by Allah until morning (Sahih al-Bukhari).

Should Ayatul Kursi be recited before sleeping?

Yes — reciting it before sleep is established from the Sunnah; the narration says that Shaytan does not come near the one who recites it until morning (Sahih al-Bukhari).

What is the English translation of Ayatul Kursi?

Allah - there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of [all] existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep... And He is the Most High, the Most Great. (Saheeh International, 2:255)
