Why is it called "Chand Ki Tarikh"?

The Islamic calendar is lunar — every month begins with the sighting of the new crescent, which is why the Hijri date is commonly called chand ki tarikh (also written Chand ki tareekh), "the date of the moon". In Pakistan the official start of each month is announced by the Central Ruet-e-Hilal Committee after the crescent is sighted. The date on this page is calculated (Umm al-Qura) and normally matches Pakistan's official date, but a one-day difference is possible after a moon-sighting announcement — for Ramadan, the Eids and other major occasions, always treat the Ruet-e-Hilal announcement and your local masjid's notice as final. When does the Islamic date change — at midnight or at Maghrib?

In Islamic practice the new day starts at Maghrib (sunset), so the next date begins after Maghrib. Civil calendars change the date at midnight; this page follows the Pakistan civil day (midnight PKT).

Why is Pakistan's Islamic date sometimes different from Saudi Arabia's?

Pakistan starts each month on the Ruet-e-Hilal Committee's moon-sighting announcement, while Saudi Arabia follows the calculated Umm al-Qura calendar, so a one-day difference is common.
